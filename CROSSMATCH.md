## 2. The contract

`js/kernels/crossmatch/` (`constants`, `geometry`, `epoch`, `match`, `population`, `record`, `packs`, `index`); never precached (`NEVER_PRECACHED`), imported by no shipped module. A **catalog** is `{id, ra, dec (degrees), epoch (Julian year, one or per row), positionStep: {raDeg, decDeg}, pmra, pmdec (mas/yr; pmra is mu_alpha*), pmraErr, pmdecErr, sigmaPosArcsec, columns}`.

**Epoch.** Positions move to `toEpoch` in straight lines in space with the radial velocity taken as zero: `r' = normalised(r + dt (mu_alpha* p + mu_delta q))` with `p` east, `q` north, `dt = toEpoch - epoch`. Pure northward motion moves the declination by exactly `atan(mu_delta dt)`; a source whose shift passes a pole turns by 180 degrees. The perspective term a radial velocity would add is neglected. Position variances per axis: `sigma0^2 + (sigma_mu dt)^2`, position and proper-motion errors taken as independent (the packs carry no correlation between them; they do carry the proper-motion correlation, which does not enter a per-axis variance). A shift over 3600 arcsec is refused; a row with no proper motion when `dt` is not zero is excluded and counted in `excluded`, never matched at an unpropagated position.

**Refusals, in the order the kernel checks them.** `input` (a position that is not a finite number, unequal columns, an empty catalog, more than 20000 rows), `epoch` (absent or not a number, for either catalog or the common epoch), `radius` (not above zero, or over **10 arcsec**), `resolution` (the catalog does not say how coarsely its positions are stored), `rounded-positions` (half the largest stored step, on the sky, is more than **a quarter of the radius**), `proper-motion` (no proper motions and not at the common epoch), `shift-too-large`. The packs state their epoch in the frame string and their rounding in the reductions prose; `catalogFromPack` reads both from the record and returns null for either when the record does not state it, so a pack that states neither is refused rather than matched.

**Match.** Each row of A is paired with the nearest row of B within the radius (`nearest`: several rows of A may share one row of B, `shared` counts the rows of B so used; ties to the lower index of B) or by increasing separation with each row used once (`oneToOne`: ties to the lower index of A). A row of A with another candidate in the radius is counted as `ambiguous`. A pair carries the distance, the epoch difference (A minus B) and, if both catalogs gave position errors, the distance's uncertainty along the separation.

**False matches.** The shifted-match test, stated from its definition (the published description is request R3): B is moved on the sky by a distance (20 radii by default) along 8 bearings (from north through east) and matched again; the rows of A with a shifted row of B in the radius are chance coincidences, because a shift removes every counterpart. With a footprint circle of B only the rows of A inside the shifted circle (by the radius) are counted, so the field's edge does not bias the count low (without one the result says so). `p_hat = sum C_k / sum E_k` with an exact Poisson (Garwood) interval of `sum C_k` over `sum E_k`; the false-match fraction is `p_hat` times the rows of A over the number of matches, capped at 1. **It is an upper bound on the false fraction whenever counterparts exist** (a chance coincidence is a false match only if no nearer true partner exists) and equal to it for rows with none; in a world with counterparts the measured bound was 4 times the realized false fraction (E6). A `p_hat` above 0.2 makes the status `unreliable`: the radius is too large for the density.

**Populations.** `distribution(values, {edges})` counts in bins with the statistics kernel's `poissonInterval` of each count; `compareSamples(x, y)` is the difference of two means through `difference()` of Prompt 154 in its valid class: two independent samples of at least 30 values each, finite, each mean roughly normal. A smaller sample is refused (`sample-size`), constant samples are `degenerate`, nothing gets a p value outside the class.

**Record.** `joinedTable(result, a, b, {passA, passB})` returns columns (`aIndex`, `bIndex`, `distance` arcsec, `epochDifference` yr, `distanceSigma` when known, `a.<id>`, `b.<id>`); `crossmatchRecord` is a `gravitas.artifact` envelope (version 2 with the interval kinds) with the radius (assumed), the counts, the chance probability and the false-match fraction with their intervals, and two quantities per pair. The common epoch is in `made`.

## 6. Limits a consumer must state

1. Straight-line motion with radial velocity zero. A source with a large radial velocity at a small distance has an unmodelled perspective term; the 3600 arcsec cap bounds nothing about it.
2. The epoch of the SDSS pack is the **declared** `J2000`, not each object's observation epoch. Request R1 has since been fetched (section 10): the pack's rows were observed on three nights in 2002 (J2002.04 to J2002.93, a spread of 0.89 yr), and the pair set with those epochs is identical to the declared-epoch set (D4b: 0 pairs differ). For clusters (proper motions of a few mas/yr) it does not change the pair set; for field stars with 100 mas/yr and a spread of years it would.
3. The false-match estimate is for chance coincidences of the radius in the density of the field, not for blends or for a counterpart that is the wrong object; it is an upper bound where counterparts exist; the shifted-match test assumes the density is locally the same after a shift, which a clustered field violates at the scale of the shift (the packs' 20 arcsec shifts are small against the structure, and the result is reported with its footprint).
4. The packs' positions are rounded to 0.00001 degree (0.04 arcsec) and carry no positional errors, so the match distance of a pack pair has no uncertainty (`sigmaArcsec` is null).
5. The two-sample comparison is for means of independent samples of at least 30; it says nothing of the shapes of the distributions.

**The citation of the shifted-match test (R3).** Wilson and Naylor 2018, section 4.2.3, estimate the false-match level of a Gaia to WISE match by moving the Gaia sources of a Galactic box by 10 degrees in longitude, matching them to the original WISE catalogue, and counting what matches (about 0.6 percent of the WISE catalogue with their probability-based match against 4 percent expected from a nearest-neighbour match). That is the same idea as the kernel's: a shifted copy has no true counterparts, so what matches is chance. The kernel's own scheme (20 radii along 8 bearings, an exact Poisson interval, the footprint correction) is not theirs, and their match is not a nearest-neighbour match; the citation supports the definition, not the numbers.
