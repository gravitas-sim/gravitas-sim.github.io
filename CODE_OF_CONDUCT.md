# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. This is a teaching tool, so a lot of the people who turn up
here are students, instructors trying something new, or someone who has never
opened a pull request before. Treat a naive question as a good-faith question,
because it almost always is.

## Standards

Things that make this a place worth participating in:

- Assuming the other person is trying to help.
- Accepting that "I was wrong about that" is a normal sentence and not a defeat.
- Criticizing the work rather than the person who did it — and being specific
  enough that the criticism is useful.
- Remembering that a beginner's question is evidence the documentation could be
  better, not evidence the beginner should have tried harder.

Things that are not acceptable, in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, or anywhere else this project happens:

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks.
- Demeaning or discriminatory remarks of any kind, including about someone's
  background, identity, or level of experience.
- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of a sexual nature.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption — derailing threads, reopening settled decisions to make
  a point, or badgering a maintainer for a response.

## Scope

This applies in every space this project uses, and when someone is representing
the project elsewhere.

## Enforcement

Report a problem to Carl Ziegler at <Carl.Ziegler@sfasu.edu>. Reports are
handled privately and the reporter's identity is not shared with the person
reported.

I will read it, decide, and say what I decided. Depending on what happened, that
might be a private word, an edited or removed comment, or a block from the
repository. I am one maintainer with a teaching load, so I will not always be
fast, but I will answer.

If the person you need to report is me, say so to someone at the Department of
Physics, Engineering and Astronomy at Stephen F. Austin State University. A code
of conduct whose only channel is the maintainer is not much use when the
maintainer is the problem.
